Chapter IV. The Catalans

A hundred paces from the spot where the two friends sat, drinking their wine under the trellis-work, was the village of the Catalans.

For centuries this enigmatic colony had preserved its unadulterated customs, speech, and isolation from the people of Marseilles. They had landed on the shores of Provence and established a fortress of fishermen, maintaining their traditions unaltered.

Inside one of the whitewashed cottages, bathed in the violet dusk of the Mediterranean, sat Mercédès. Her eyes, deep and mournful, gazed intently at the horizon where the sun sank like an incandescent galleon into the sea.

"Edmond," she murmured softly, her fingers tightening upon her breast, "the vessel has entered the roadstead, and yet you have not come."

Fernand stood before her, watchful, consuming her grief with a jealousy that burned colder than treason. He saw that every heartbeat of this girl belonged irrevocably to Dantès.
